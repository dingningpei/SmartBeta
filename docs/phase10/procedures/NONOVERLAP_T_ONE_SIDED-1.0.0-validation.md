## 1. Primary source and equations

The procedure is the **one-sample Student t-test applied to non-overlapping
observations** — the classical one-sample t-test (Student, 1908) used on a
series whose adjacent observations are structurally non-overlapping because
the holding horizon equals the rebalance spacing. It is the candidate frozen by
PA-0; the documented alternative (not implemented here) is the
equal-weighted-cosine HAR t-test of Lazarus, Lewis, Stock & Watson (2018).

Let `x = (x_1, ..., x_T)` be the per-formation net long-short return series,
`s in {+1, -1}` the precommitted direction, and `theta' = s * theta` the
direction-adjusted estimand. With `z_t = s * x_t`:

- `xbar = (1/T) * sum_t z_t` (sample mean);
- `sd = sqrt( (1/(T-1)) * sum_t (z_t - xbar)^2 )` (sample sd, `ddof = 1`);
- `se = sd / sqrt(T)`;
- `t = sqrt(T) * xbar / sd = xbar / se`;
- `p = 1 - F_{t,T-1}(t)` (one-sided, `ASYMPTOTIC` validity);
- `UB = xbar + t_{1-bound_alpha, T-1} * sd / sqrt(T)`
  (one-sided upper confidence bound at level `1 - bound_alpha`).

The null is `H0: theta' <= 0`. The p-value is exact under i.i.d. normal
returns and asymptotically valid under the declared dependence assumptions.

## 2. Assumptions (declared, recorded, not certified by Phase 10)

Dependence-assumptions text (hashed into the contract):

> The per-period net long-short returns are weakly dependent with first-order autocorrelation |rho_1| <= 0.2 and finite fourth moment. Non-overlap is structural: the holding horizon equals the rebalance spacing (h = 21 sessions). Recorded, not certified by Phase 10.

Sample requirements: `T >= 24` complete periods, sample
standard deviation `> 0`, all values finite. Missingness:
`COMPLETE_REQUIRED` (the series index must equal the expected HOLDOUT
formation-date index). The only parameter is `bound_alpha in (0, 0.5)`; there
is no bandwidth, no lag choice and no tuning.

- Grid: `T in {24, 30, 36, 48, 60}`; `rho_1 in {0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3}`;

  (reported only, not gating).

### Data-generating process (the estimand's construction)

The per-formation series is the net long-short return of two equal quintile
legs of `N` names. Each name obeys a one-factor model with a unit-variance
common factor `f` and idiosyncratic noise `eps ~ N(0, 1)`; the long-short legs
carry a unit net exposure to `f`, and the idiosyncratic noise averages over
the `N/5` names of each leg. The net long-short return is therefore

    LS_t = (delta + f_t + u_t) / sqrt(1 + v),   u_t ~ N(0, v),  v = 10 / N,

where `f` is a stationary unit-variance AR(1) with coefficient `rho_1` and the
cell's innovation distribution, and `v = 10/N` is the exact variance of
(mean of one quintile leg - mean of the other) of unit-variance idiosyncratic
noise. The `1/sqrt(1+v)` normalization sets the series variance to one, so
`delta = sharpe / sqrt(12)` is the mean that realises an annual Sharpe of
`sharpe` at unit monthly volatility. The reduction of the quintile long-short
to `f + u` is exact in distribution (leg means of i.i.d. Gaussian noise are
Gaussian) and is checked against a literal `N`-name simulation in the test
suite (`test_mc_reduction_matches_literal_name_simulation`).

- **Result:** the in-region acceptance criteria **FAIL**. Verdict:
  **ADMISSION_BLOCKER**; the procedure is **NOT CERTIFIED** at the frozen
  tolerance.

The failure is the classical first-order autocorrelation size distortion of
the one-sample t-test: the sample variance estimates the marginal variance,
not the long-run variance, so positive serial correlation inflates the
statistic. For an AR(1) with coefficient `rho_1`, the variance of `sqrt(T)*xbar`
is inflated by `1 + 2*sum_{k=1}^{T-1} (1 - k/T) rho_1^k`; at `rho_1 = 0.2`
and `T = 36` this is about `1.48` (`sd` inflation about `1.22`), which lifts
the one-sided 5% rejection rate to roughly `0.08`. This is a property of the
declared assumption region, not of the DGP's finer details: any series whose
first-order autocorrelation is near `rho_1` exhibits it.

## 6. Limitations

- **The declared region is not met.** Under the directly realised declared
  assumption (`rho_1` = the first-order autocorrelation of the net long-short
  series), the procedure over-rejects for `rho_1 >= 0.1` and its bound
  under-covers. The frozen fallback is the EWC HAR candidate, which needs its
  own PA-0.
- The validation is ASYMPTOTIC in the sense of the contract; it is a finite
  Monte Carlo over declared DGPs, not a proof.
- The DGP realizes the estimand's cross-sectional construction exactly in
  distribution; the net factor exposure of the long-short legs is set to one.
  A construction whose long-short nets out the common factor would be less
  autocorrelated, but that is outside the declared `rho_1` region, and the
  procedure would then not be stress-tested at its stated boundary.
- No claim is made about cross-sectional dependence beyond the simulated
  construction, about real data, or about any hypothesis, data set or alpha.
